On a reference-answer question-answering benchmark, some judges aligned well with people, but the best still fell below human-human agreement. A simple substring baseline outperformed several model evaluators.

Yan also highlights the extra discrimination provided by chance-adjusted agreement. A sophisticated model should justify its cost against straightforward alternatives when the actual task is mostly recognizing equivalent answer text.
