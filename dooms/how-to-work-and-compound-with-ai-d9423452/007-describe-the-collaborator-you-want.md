Yan uses global instructions to describe how he wants the assistant to behave: communicate directly, admit uncertainty, investigate failures and keep changes focused. These are examples of his preferences, not a universal specification.

Writing such expectations down turns repeated corrections into reusable guidance. The aim is to make the assistant's behavior predictable enough to support productive collaboration.
