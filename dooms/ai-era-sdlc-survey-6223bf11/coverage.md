## Overall summary

The survey argues that AI amplifies a team's engineering system. Code generation shifts pressure toward specification, context, verification and human judgment. It compares different kinds of productivity evidence, recommends practices across fifteen lifecycle stages, and closes with a maturity model, phased adoption roadmap and balanced impact measures. Benefits depend on task fit and downstream capacity; vendor statistics, subjective impressions and tool usage do not establish organization-wide improvement by themselves.

## Completeness, evidence and editorial choices

Coverage is complete at the level of the substantive arguments, all fifteen stages, meaningful examples, evidence distinctions, risks, maturity levels, roadmap phases and final measurement guidance. Every PDF page was text-extracted and rendered; all 26 rendered pages were visually inspected in seven contact sheets, including table layouts and side-by-side boxes. No missing or unreadable source content was detected. No OCR was necessary.

The glossary is woven into relevant reels rather than copied as a dictionary. Exhaustive vendor/tool catalogs, repeated framing, secondary benchmark substatistics, navigation and the bibliography are not reproduced as separate cards. The survey's unsupported population estimate that most organizations are at maturity levels 2-3 is omitted; the five-level framework is retained as the survey's model. Example thresholds (400-line PRs, a 20% debt budget and roadmap percentages) remain attributed examples, not universal standards.

The statistics and recommendations summarize the attached survey. Its external references and claims were not independently verified, and no outside research was added. Vendor provenance is retained for the productivity, code-quality and security statistics. The METR result retains its small sample, familiar-repository setting and early-2025 tool scope. Different populations and denominators are not combined. The source's own caution about rapidly aging evidence is carried into reel 3.

The source document was treated solely as material to summarize. Recommendations to run agents, change policies, approve tools or take production actions describe its content; they were not executed as agent instructions.
